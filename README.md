## Problem Title
SIH 26033: Multiple intermediaries reduce farmers earnings and increase consumer prices.

## Problem Creater's Organization
Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food & Public Distribution

## Theme
Agriculture, FoodTech & Rural Development

## Problem Description
Expected Solution: Create a digital marketplace that:

• Connects farmers/FPOs directly with consumers and bulk buyers.
• Provides logistics support.
• Uses AI for demand forecasting and route optimization.

Benefits:

• Better prices for farmers.
• Lower prices for consumers.
• Reduced supply chain inefficiencies.

## Proposed Solution
FarmCircle is a smart agricultural marketplace designed to create a direct connection between farmers and customers without depending on multiple intermediaries. The platform introduces a unique feature called "Collective Harvest Circles", where farmers growing similar crops in nearby locations can form temporary groups and sell their products together. Customers can place advance orders, compare products and select their preferred farmers. The system also provides a digital harvest card that displays the crop type, farm location, harvesting date and available quantity. If a farmer has excess produce, the platform automatically helps identify nearby buyers who may need those products. This solution addresses the problem of low farmer income and high consumer prices by reducing unnecessary supply-chain stages. Its uniqueness lies in combining group selling, advance booking and surplus-product matching in one platform, creating a more transparent and cooperative agricultural marketplace.
## Technical Approach
FarmCircle can be developed as a web-based application using HTML, CSS and JavaScript for the frontend and Python with Django for backend development. MySQL can be used to store farmer profiles, customer details, crop information, orders and payment records. The implementation begins with user registration, where farmers and buyers create separate accounts. Farmers can then upload crop details, available quantities, prices and harvest dates. Customers can search for products and place advance orders. A location-based matching system groups nearby farmers who sell similar crops, while an AI-based recommendation module can analyse previous orders and customer demand to suggest suitable buyers. The platform can also include a delivery management module to coordinate shared transportation. The complete process follows a simple flow: Registration → Product Listing → Customer Demand → Farmer Group Matching → Order Confirmation → Shared Delivery → Successful Purchase.
## Feasibility and Viability
FarmCircle is a feasible solution because it can be developed using commonly available programming languages, open-source frameworks and existing digital technologies. A basic prototype can initially be created with farmer registration, product listing, customer ordering and group matching features without requiring expensive hardware. However, some challenges may include limited digital knowledge among farmers, internet connectivity problems in rural areas, difficulties in coordinating transportation and maintaining product quality during delivery. These challenges can be managed by providing a simple interface in regional languages, allowing assistance through local agricultural centres and using SMS notifications where internet access is limited. Transportation can be coordinated through nearby farmer groups, while product quality can be maintained through basic verification and delivery tracking. The platform can initially be tested in one agricultural area and gradually expanded to other regions. Therefore, FarmCircle is technically achievable, economically practical for gradual development and suitable for long-term implementation.
## Impact and Benefits
FarmCircle can create a positive impact on farmers, consumers and the agricultural community. Farmers can receive better returns by selling their products directly to customers and sharing transportation expenses with nearby farmers. Consumers can purchase fresh agricultural products at more reasonable prices with clear information about their origin. Small-scale farmers can gain better market access by joining collective selling groups instead of depending on individual buyers. The surplus-product matching feature can reduce the chances of agricultural products remaining unsold and going to waste. Economically, the platform can support rural income and reduce unnecessary distribution expenses. Socially, it encourages cooperation among farmers and builds trust between producers and consumers. Environmentally, shared transportation and better demand planning can help reduce unnecessary journeys and food wastage. Overall, FarmCircle aims to create a fairer, more affordable and sustainable agricultural supply chain.
## Research and References
The development of FarmCircle is supported by research into existing agricultural marketing systems and digital farming initiatives. The Government of India's e-NAM (National Agriculture Market) platform provides online agricultural trading and connects farmers with different buyers. Its features demonstrate how digital platforms can improve market access, price transparency and agricultural trading. FarmCircle takes inspiration from these existing systems and extends the concept through collective harvest groups, advance customer orders and surplus-product matching. Additional research into digital agriculture, farmer producer organisations and supply-chain management can help improve the proposed platform. These references provide a foundation for designing a practical and technology-based solution for farmers.
